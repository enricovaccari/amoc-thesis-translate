Living Atlantic

Scene I — The Column · About this visualization

An experiential, data-driven representation of the Atlantic Meridional Overturning Circulation (AMOC) at 26.5°N. This page explains — transparently — what the visualization is, which data it rests on, and how machine learning is used to make an invisible ocean system perceptually explorable.

A · Ethics & philosophy

Why this visualization exists

The AMOC is a planetary-scale circulation that redistributes heat across the Atlantic. It is largely invisible and abstract: normally reduced to a single number of Sverdrups on a line chart. Such conventional formats treat the system as an object to read rather than a dynamic to inhabit. This visualization asks whether making the structure perceivable — as a living vertical column with direction, depth and rhythm — can support a more grounded, embodied understanding of a complex, slow, high-stakes climate system.

Making the invisible intelligible

By mapping transport, its vertical structure and its temporal variability onto motion, position, colour and sound, the scene exploits pre-conceptual grounding (the up/down, near/far, strong/weak image schemas) so that a viewer can feel where the current is strong and northward and where it is weak and returning — before any conscious reading of the axes.

The role of experiential scientific communication

Experiential visualization is not a replacement for charts; it is a complementary layer. It aims to build intuition, curiosity and a sense of scale, and to invite exploration — turning a static readout into something a non-specialist can interrogate.

Interpretive limits

This visualization does not predict the future. It does not replace physical climate models. It transforms observed structures and dynamics of the system into a form that can be perceptually explored. Every on-screen quantity is traceable to a dataset value or an explicitly declared computational transformation — nothing is invented for effect.
B · Datasets

Where the data comes from

Primary observations — RAPID array (26.5°N)

The vertical AMOC structure is built from the RAPID–MOCHA–WBTS continuous monitoring array at 26.5°N — the reference observational record of Atlantic overturning transport. It provides the depth-resolved overturning streamfunction Ψ(z) that the on-screen column represents, spanning the observational period 2004–2024.

Supporting data — Copernicus reanalysis ensemble

A reanalysis ensemble (Copernicus / GREP-style, C-GLORS · GLORYS · ORAS5) is used as a supporting source for the ensemble spread — the disagreement between members — which provides an empirical, data-grounded measure of uncertainty rather than an invented one.

Variables represented

  • Ψ(z) — overturning streamfunction vs depth (Sverdrups), 307 levels, 0–~6000 m.
  • Local velocity — derived as dΨ/dz, giving the true northward/southward flow direction at each depth.
  • Temporal variability — monthly evolution across 2004–2024.
  • Uncertainty — ensemble spread from the reanalysis members.

Observed vs computed — a clear separation

Directly observed
  • RAPID overturning transport at 26.5°N
  • Depth grid of the section
  • Reanalysis ensemble members (support)
Computational transformation
  • PCA decomposition & two-mode reconstruction
  • Local velocity as the derivative dΨ/dz
  • Anomaly scoring (Isolation Forest)
  • Interpolation & visual/sonic encoding

The moving column is a two-mode reconstruction, not a replay of raw sensor traces: it is what remains when only the dominant learned modes are kept. This is stated openly so the viewer never mistakes a computed representation for a direct measurement.

C · Machine learning

How ML is used

Machine learning here is a layer of extraction, organization and perceptual translation — not a physical model of the ocean and not a forecaster.

  • Latent structure extraction. PCA compresses hundreds of correlated depth levels into a low-dimensional latent space, exposing the hidden coordinates that organize the system.
  • Dominant modes. The first two empirical orthogonal functions (EOF1, EOF2) capture the principal ways the column varies: EOF1 ≈ whole-column overturning intensity; EOF2 ≈ vertical redistribution. Two modes reconstruct the large majority of all variability.
  • Dimensionality reduction. The full profile at any month is rebuilt as mean + PC1(t)·EOF1 + PC2(t)·EOF2. The animation is a trajectory through this learned latent space, not a re-play of observations.
  • Anomaly detection. An Isolation Forest flags states that depart from the learned regime — candidate anomalies in the system's behaviour.
  • Latent-space transitions. Movement between states is expressed as motion in the reduced representation, making regime shifts legible.
  • Uncertainty. The reanalysis ensemble spread is used as a data-derived structure of confidence rather than a decorative flourish.
Machine learning does not replace physical knowledge of the system. It is used as a layer that extracts, organizes and perceptually translates the data — surfacing hidden structure so that it can be seen, heard and explored.
D · Design grammar

From system property to percept

Each scientific property is mapped to the perceptual channel best suited to carry it, following the graphical-perception and uncertainty-cognition literature. The ordering of channels by effectiveness (position > length > motion > colour > texture) drives which property gets which channel.

PrincipleSystem propertyPerceptual implementation & cognitive rationale
P0 Relative importance of variables The most important variables are assigned the most effective perceptual channels. Channel-effectiveness ranking (Bertin 1967; Cleveland & McGill 1984; Munzner 2014): expressiveness/effectiveness — encode what matters most with position and motion, not colour hue.
P1 Magnitude / intensity AMOC intensity (max Ψ) encoded through position and scale on the column. Position and length are the most accurately decoded channels for quantitative magnitude (Cleveland & McGill 1984; Munzner 2014).
P2 Dynamics / change over time Particle motion represents temporal dynamics and flow direction. Motion is a powerful pre-attentive channel; velocity reads as magnitude without a legend, and animation aids understanding of change (Ware 2021; Sterman 2008 on inhabiting vs reading a dynamic system).
P3 Anomaly / departure from regime Local changes in density, colour or texture highlight anomalies. Local feature contrast pops out pre-attentively (Ware 2021); framing departures from a baseline supports correct anomaly reasoning (Padilla et al. 2018).
P4 Uncertainty Dynamic variability — movement, density and system behaviour — expresses uncertainty. Frequency/ensemble and animated representations of uncertainty are decoded more faithfully than static error bars (Hullman et al. 2015; Kale et al. 2019; Padilla et al. 2018).
P5 Secondary ordered variables Sound carries secondary variables without overloading the visual channel. Sonification offloads ordered information to an under-used modality; rhythm maps to intensity (Hermann, Hunt & Neuhoff 2011).
